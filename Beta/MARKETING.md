## FitAI 60-Day Marketing Plan

**Goal:**  
Use 60 days to prove that:
- Real lifters can use fit.ai in the gym without friction.
- 50–100 people try it, 10–20 use it 3–4+ times, and 3–5 say "I'd pay for this."
- There is a clear, repeatable way to get users via 1–2 channels.

This doc is a working plan for distribution and storytelling, not a rigid script.

---

## 1. Positioning & Avatar

### 1.1 Product (freeze for 60 days)

What we are selling for this phase:

- **AI coach + logger + calendar + insights**, specifically:
  - Chat that remembers workouts, preferences, and injuries.
  - Structured workout logging (sets/reps/weights) that feels smooth in the gym.
  - Calendar + weekly strip that shows consistency, intensity, and PR days.
  - Journey-aware insights that compare you to your own Day 1 and track progression.

For 60 days:

- Only ship:
  - UX/jank fixes,
  - Stability/performance improvements,
  - Small quality-of-life tweaks and insight improvements.
- **No big new feature branches** (calories, programs, social, etc.) unless they are obviously critical.

### 1.2 Target user (avatar)

Primary avatar:

- Age: ~20–35
- Trains: **3–5x/week**
- Goal: get stronger / build muscle / improve physique
- Behaviors:
  - Uses phone in the gym already (YouTube, notes, logging apps).
  - Has tried other logging apps or notes, but drops them after a few weeks.
  - Likes tech and is willing to try AI tools if they feel genuinely useful.

Language/tone:

- Talk like a lifter who codes, not like a generic SaaS founder.
- Examples:
  - "Bench is up 7.5kg since Day 1, Bulgarians finally moving."
  - "Today was a heavy day; fit.ai called me out on training 6 of the last 8 days."

### 1.3 Core promise

> **"fit.ai is an AI coach that remembers every workout and conversation, and shows you how you're actually progressing."**

Not:
- "Another fitness app"
- "Just a logger"

Emphasize:

- Memory (never forgets your journey).
- Insights (turns stats into coaching).
- Smooth in-gym experience (logging + checking insights + chat).

## 2. Channel Strategy: One Product, One Avatar, One Channel

Borrowing from Hormozi's idea: **one product, one avatar, one main channel** until there is clear signal.

### 2.1 Primary channel: Twitter / X

This is the **main stage** for:

- Daily updates.
- Training logs + screenshots.
- Build-in-public moments.
- Connection with early users.

Cadence: **4–7 posts per week**.

Types of posts:

1. **Training logs + screenshots** (core)
2. **Build in public** (what you fixed/changed)
3. **Journey & motivation** (how it feels building and training with fit.ai)
4. **User moments** (once you have real users and quotes)

### 2.2 Supporting channel: Reddit

Purpose:

- Deeper, honest posts about using fit.ai.
- Collect feedback from serious lifters.
- Occasionally drive signups from relevant subs.

Cadence: **1–2 posts per week**.

Stick to **high-signal posts**:

- "I logged every workout with my own AI coach app for 4 weeks—here's what it got right/wrong."
- "What my AI coach noticed about my training that I didn't."

Avoid:

- Spamming "launch soon" or landing page links with no story.

## 3. Content Pillars

Everything revolves around you **actually training with fit.ai** and showing what it does.

### 3.1 Pillar 1 – Training logs + fit.ai screenshots

Purpose:

- Show that fit.ai is **real** and used in the gym.
- Show the **UI and insights** in context.
- Make people think: "I want my training to look and feel like this."

What to show:

- Session log summary card.
- Calendar view (showing heavy days vs light days vs rest).
- Journey-aware insight that felt meaningful (e.g., Day 1 vs today).

Example tweet:

> "Push day logged in fit.ai.  
> Bench is up +7.5kg since Day 1, Bulgarians +2 reps per set.  
> Calendar makes it obvious I've actually lifted 5 out of the last 7 days instead of just 'feeling' consistent."

### 3.2 Pillar 2 – Build in public (product)

Purpose:

- Show consistent progress.
- Build trust with devs/early adopters.
- Give shape to the product story (not just "AI buzzwords").

What to share:

- Simple before/after fixes:
  - "Fixed losing an in-progress workout when switching to chat."
  - "Made the calendar only tappable on days you actually trained."
- Backend/insight wins explained in simple language:
  - "Today we made insights compare you to your Day 1 baseline instead of just last session."

Example tweet:

> "Mid-session logging felt fragile—if you left to chat, you could lose your workout. Fixed it today.  
> Now you can log staggered sets, ask the AI about an exercise, and come back without losing a rep."

### 3.3 Pillar 3 – Journey & motivation

Purpose:

- Humanize you and the product.
- Make followers care about your journey, not just the app.

Topics:

- How it feels to train with your own AI coach.
- Balancing building + lifting.
- Moments when fit.ai surprised you.

Example:

> "Weirdly satisfying moment: fit.ai reminded me that my Day 1 dumbbell press was 5kg per hand.  
> Today I hit 24kg. Having that history in one place feels better than any spreadsheet I've used."

### 3.4 Pillar 4 – User stories (later)

Once 3–5 testers are actually using fit.ai:

- Anonymized quotes:
  - "This is the first time I've actually stuck to logging for 3 weeks."
  - "Seeing my heavy days on the calendar makes me want to protect rest days."
- Screenshots of their stats/insights (with consent and anonymized).

Use sparingly, but they're powerful social proof.

## 4. 60-Day Execution Plan

### 4.1 Weekly cadence (high level)

For each of the 8 weeks:

- **Training (3–5x / week)**:
  - Use fit.ai for your real workouts.
  - After each meaningful session, capture **1–2 screenshots**.
- **Twitter / X (4–7 posts / week)**:
  - 2–3 training + screenshots posts.
  - 1–2 build-in-public posts.
  - Optional: 0–1 thread with a deeper story.
- **Reddit (1–2 posts / week)**:
  - Deeper write-ups or experiments.
  - No spam; always value-first.
- **DMs / outreach (ongoing)**:
  - DM people who like/engage and seem relevant:
    - "I'm building fit.ai, an AI coach that remembers every workout.  
       If you lift 3–5x/week and want to try the beta, I can send you the link. Honest feedback welcome."

### 4.2 Week-by-week focus

#### Weeks 1–2 – "Founders using their own product"

Objective:

- Show that you and your close circle are training with fit.ai.
- Fix obvious UX pain (clunk, lost data, confusing flows).

Content focus:

- Your workouts + calendar screenshots.
- Fix logs:
  - Logging UX,
  - Calendar interaction,
  - First batch of insight prompt improvements (already done).

Target metrics (rough):

- 10–20 people sign up / try.
- 3–5 log 3+ workouts.

#### Weeks 3–4 – "Early external testers"

Objective:

- Get 5–10 non-friend testers actively using the app.
- Collect qualitative feedback: what they loved, what confused them.

Content focus:

- Share learnings from testers (anonymized).
- Show improvements you ship based on feedback.
- A longer **"30 days training with my AI coach"** thread on Twitter and a Reddit post.

Target metrics:

- 30–50 people have tried the app.
- 5–10 have logged 3+ workouts.
- At least 2–3 people explicitly say "this feels good" or "I'd pay for this."

#### Weeks 5–6 – "Sharpen the story"

Objective:

- Tighten the value proposition based on real feedback:
  - What do people actually mention as "the thing" they like?
    - Memory?
    - Insights?
    - Calendar?
    - Just the clean logging?

Content focus:

- Highlight 1–2 **strongest features** in more detail:
  - E.g. a thread just about **journey-aware insights**.
  - Or a thread about how the **calendar + weekly strip** changed how you see your training.

Target metrics:

- 50–100 people have tried it.
- 10–20 have logged 3+ workouts.
- 3–5 say they'd pay.

#### Weeks 7–8 – "Pricing story and call-to-action"

Objective:

- Start seeding the idea of **paid tiers** without forcing it.
- Make clear that early users will be **grandfathered**.

Content focus:

- Posts/threads like:
  - "If I charged for fit.ai, what would be fair?" (engagement, feedback)
  - Announce pricing intentions: free beta → $9.99/m & $89.99/y for early adopters → $14.99 later.
- Highlight people who stuck with it >4 weeks and what changed for them.

Target metrics:

- 10+ people who are clear active users.
- At least a few who say "this is worth $X" (and preferably $10+).

## 5. Landing, Onboarding & Screens

### 5.1 Website messaging (high level)

Core narrative:

- "AI coach that remembers every workout and conversation."
- "Log your training, see real stats and insights, and talk to a coach that actually knows your history."

Key points to show:

- Chat screenshot where it references past workouts.
- Workout log screenshot.
- Calendar + stats screenshot.
- Example insight (Day 1 vs today).

CTA during beta:

- "Start free beta" (no credit card).

### 5.2 Onboarding prompts

Onboarding should:

- Quickly capture:
  - Primary goal (build muscle, get lean, get consistent).
  - Experience (beginner/intermediate/advanced).
  - Preferred training style (home/gym, PPL, full-body, etc.).
- Then immediately:
  - Log first workout OR
  - Ask a few meaningful questions in chat.

You already have most of this wired; the focus for marketing is to **show screenshots of this flow**.

## 6. Measurement & Feedback

### 6.1 Simple metrics to track (manual is fine)

Create a simple spreadsheet or Notion table with:

- **Acquisition:**
  - Source: Twitter, Reddit, DM, friend, etc.
  - Date joined.
- **Activation:**
  - Onboarding completed (Y/N).
  - First workout logged (Y/N).
- **Engagement:**
  - Workouts logged (per week).
  - Chat messages (rough count or "uses chat Y/N").
- **Retention:**
  - Came back in Week 2? Week 3?
- **Sentiment:**
  - Free-text quotes.
  - "Would you pay?" and if yes, how much.

You don't need a full analytics stack for this phase; 20–50 rows of honest data is enough to see patterns.

### 6.2 Qualitative feedback

Ask testers:

- "What's the most useful thing fit.ai does for you right now?"
- "What's confusing or annoying?"
- "If I removed one feature today, which one would you miss the most?"
- "If I charged for this, what would feel fair?"

Keep the raw answers. These will feed:

- Website copy,
- Pricing decisions,
- Product priorities (what to deepen vs ignore).

## 7. Pricing Story (for later, but seeded now)

Target pricing strategy:

- **Beta:** Free (full access).
- **Early adopters (grandfathered):**
  - $9.99/month
  - $89.99/year
  - "Locked in for life" for people who join early and give feedback.
- **Later (public list price):**
  - $14.99/month (and a higher annual).

How to talk about it:

- During beta:
  - "Free while in beta; people who help shape this will get a lower locked-in price."
- As you approach paid:
  - "We're moving to $14.99/m, but early users who joined before [date] will keep $9.99/m as long as they stay subscribed."

---

## 8. Risks & Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Trying too many channels at once:**
  - Stick to Twitter/X as home base, Reddit as support. Don't dilute yourself across TikTok, Instagram, LinkedIn, etc. yet.
- **Feature-chasing instead of loop-chasing:**
  - Focus on: log workout → see stats/insights → chat → next workout.
- **Over-focusing on vanity metrics (likes, impressions):**
  - Measure active users and repeat usage instead.
- **Going silent:**
  - Missing a day or two is fine; disappearing for 2–3 weeks kills momentum.

---

## 9. Summary

For the next 60 days:

- **Train with fit.ai** and show it.
- **Talk like a lifter**, not a generic AI founder.
- Use **Twitter/X daily** and **Reddit weekly** to share real progress and learnings.
- Track a small number of users closely and listen hard to what they actually love and what they ignore.

If you stay consistent on those basics, you'll know at the end of 60 days whether fit.ai has real traction potential and which levers to pull next.
